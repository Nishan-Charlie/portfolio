State Vectors & Probability Amplitudes

Visualize a quantum state as complex probability amplitudes and connect vector entries to measurement outcomes.

Intermediate16–18 min readsoftware engineering · quantum computing

A state vector is an indexed lookup table

For n qubits, the vector has 2ⁿ entries. Each index corresponds to a basis bitstring. For two qubits, the entries correspond to |00⟩, |01⟩, |10⟩, and |11⟩ if your ordering is documented.

state = np.array([0.5+0j, 0.5j, -0.5+0j, 0.5j])
probabilities = np.abs(state) ** 2

for bits, amplitude, probability in zip(
    ['00', '01', '10', '11'], state, probabilities
):
    print(bits, amplitude, probability)

An amplitude can be negative or complex; a probability cannot. Use pᵢ = |αᵢ|², and check that probabilities sum to one.

Phase is invisible—until it is not

(|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2 and (|0⟩ − |1⟩)/√2 both measure 50/50 in the computational basis. Apply H and the difference becomes visible: one returns zero with certainty, the other one.

Make the picture useful

A histogram is for measurement outcomes. Real and imaginary bar charts are for debugging. A phase-aware plot is for interference. Choose the visualization that answers your question.

Sanity checks: the vector length is 2ⁿ, the norm is one, probabilities are non-negative, and basis ordering is explicit.
